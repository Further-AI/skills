# FMCSA and vehicle reference

## Vehicle weight classes (by gross vehicle weight rating, GVWR)
NHTSA's decode returns GVWR as one of these classes. Commercial auto rating generally groups them as light (1–3), medium (4–6) and heavy (7–8).

| Class | GVWR | Typical vehicles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | up to 6,000 lb | cars, small pickups, small vans |
| 2 | 6,001–10,000 lb | full-size pickups, cargo vans |
| 3 | 10,001–14,000 lb | heavy-duty pickups, walk-in vans |
| 4 | 14,001–16,000 lb | large walk-in vans, box trucks |
| 5 | 16,001–19,500 lb | bucket trucks, large box trucks |
| 6 | 19,501–26,000 lb | single-axle straight trucks, school buses |
| 7 | 26,001–33,000 lb | refuse trucks, city tractors |
| 8 | over 33,000 lb | tractor-trailers, dump trucks, cement mixers |

## When FMCSA rules apply
- **USDOT number:** required for vehicles in interstate commerce over 10,000 lb GVWR (Class 3 and up), passenger vehicles carrying 9+ people for pay or 16+ people, or any vehicle hauling placarded hazardous materials. Many states also require one for intrastate carriers.
- **Operating authority (MC number):** required for for-hire carriers moving regulated freight or passengers across state lines. Private carriers hauling their own goods need a USDOT number but not authority.
- **CDL:** required for vehicles over 26,000 lb GVWR (Class 7 and up), combinations over 26,000 lb towing more than 10,000 lb, 16+ passenger vehicles, and placarded hazmat. Drivers of these vehicles should have a CDL on their MVR.
- **MCS-150:** the carrier's registration update, due every 2 years. FMCSA can deactivate a USDOT number when it isn't filed.

## Reading the SAFER Company Snapshot
| Section | What to take from it |
|---|---|
| Entity type, USDOT status, out-of-service date | Carrier vs. broker; whether the DOT number is active or under an out-of-service order |
| Operating authority status | Authorized for hire / not authorized; a pending or revoked authority |
| MCS-150 form date, mileage (year) | How current the registration is, and annual miles |
| Power units, drivers | The fleet size FMCSA has on file |
| Operation classification, carrier operation | Authorized for hire, private property, exempt for hire; interstate or intrastate |
| Cargo carried | Compare with the application, e.g. hazmat, livestock, general freight |
| Inspections, last 24 months | Counts and out-of-service percentages for vehicle, driver and hazmat, **with the national averages printed alongside** |
| Crashes, last 24 months | Fatal, injury and tow-away counts |
| Safety rating | Satisfactory / Conditional / Unsatisfactory / not rated, with the rating date |

## Safety signals
- **Unsatisfactory rating:** the carrier is barred from operating commercial vehicles in interstate commerce once the rating takes effect.
- **Conditional rating:** a compliance review found deficiencies that haven't been resolved.
- **Not rated:** common for smaller carriers and not a negative on its own.
- **Out-of-service rates:**
  - Compare only with the national averages on the same snapshot; they change over time.
  - Treat fewer than about 10 inspections as too few to judge.
- **Crash ratio:** crashes ÷ power units over 24 months. Compare carriers of similar operation; long-haul tractors run far more miles than local straight trucks.
- **Name or DOT changes:** a new DOT number at the same address or with the same officers as an older carrier can be a "chameleon carrier" re-registering to shed a poor record. Flag it when the snapshot or submission hints at it.
